# F4 / NormPair / economic unconditionality boundary

The active monolith is being kept as a closed theorem core: theorem claims in the retained F4/NormPair path are built from proof terms already present in the monolith, while economic existence routes remain explicit condition gates rather than being silently promoted to consequences.

Closed path:

Canonical learner / exact scan laws
→ closed composition algebra
→ exact F4 optimizer stability
→ exact NormPair quotient/factor transition
→ closed F4 × NormPair unconditional factor stability.

A separate closed negative path is:

exact F4 law
→ unit-forcing growth ray
→ no unconditional infinite-horizon F4 upper bound.

This matters because the canonical F4 coordinate is exact integer-valued. Step stability does not establish analytic coercivity, compactness, or a global infinite-horizon bound.

The economic existence path is intentionally shown with explicit gates:

closed F4 × NormPair factor stability
→ required economic interpretation/state isomorphism
→ required policy-step correctness where a policy-driven update is claimed
→ required convergence/fixed-point/market-clearing bridge
→ required economic equilibrium/supporting-price assumptions
→ generalized Walrasian existence claim.

Those gates are not closed consequences of the learner dynamics. In particular, exact representation or injectivity does not by itself produce market clearing, convergence, a fixed point, or an equilibrium witness.

The monolith also contains a closed singleton generalized-Walrasian countermodel with an empty equilibrium predicate. That supplies the universal impossibility statement that no theorem of the form “every generalized Walrasian contract has an equilibrium witness” can be constructed without additional assumptions.

Stationarity remains a separate mathematical layer. The deterministic full learner has a strictly increasing LCB total-count invariant, so it has no fixed full state; that fact alone does not establish nonexistence of invariant probability measures. Any stationary-distribution theorem needs explicit probability semantics and an invariant-measure proof.

The graph therefore distinguishes three things rather than conflating them:

1. closed exact algebra/stability results;
2. closed impossibility/countermodel results;
3. external economic conditions required before an existence theorem can be stated as a valid implication.
